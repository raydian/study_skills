# Chapter package workflow

## Main guided-reading article

Write an article, not a report. Use this narrative sequence flexibly:

1. Open with the problem or tension that makes the chapter worth reading.
2. Name the chapter's actual question.
3. Walk through how the author moves from observation/problem to concepts and claims.
4. Explain difficult concepts in plain language without changing attribution.
5. Examine the evidence, case, figure, table, formula, or code that carries the reasoning.
6. Reconstruct the argument chain and identify assumptions.
7. Show what is memorable, what is easy to misunderstand, and where the claim stops.
8. Translate implications into realistic use while marking editorial/AI additions.
9. Connect the chapter backward, forward, and to the whole book.
10. End with a genuine question or transition, not a generic summary.

Assign each material statement a reading claim ID such as `r-ch03-014` and add `[source: ch03-p028, ch03-fig004; pages: 076-077]`. Style may hide locators in publishing, but the canonical `reading.md` must retain them.

## Topic readings

Each topic reading must have one independent reader question, at least two substantive source units, a distinct thesis, and no more than 30% overlap in primary source refs with another topic article unless the overlap is explicitly necessary. The main reading remains the chapter map and does not become a teaser.

## Annotated Markdown

Quote or point to Source, then add typed callouts. Do not blend explanation into the quotation. Keep explanations concise enough for retrieval.

## Structured files

Use the annotation and knowledge schemas. Carry limitation and counterexample links into any Method, Framework, Principle, or Rule.
